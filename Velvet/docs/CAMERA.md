# Camera and encrypted movie pipeline

## Implemented path

`CameraController` owns UI state on the main actor. `CaptureEngine` confines capture session configuration, camera settings, sample delivery, and recording state to a serial user-initiated queue. An unlocked vault is required before requesting camera permission, starting a session, or changing its configuration. Microphone permission is requested only when beginning an audio-enabled video. Denied microphone access permits a silent movie and updates the microphone control.

Photos use `AVCapturePhotoOutput.fileDataRepresentation()` and preserve the returned HEIF, JPEG or DNG bytes unchanged. RAW mode requests RAW plus a processed JPEG and saves the two originals as separate vault entries. ProRAW is enabled only when the active output reports support; otherwise a supported Bayer RAW format is used. No Photos save or file-backed capture API is used. The maximum photo dimensions come from the active format and are requested on both the output and settings. This is an upper bound, not a guarantee of 24/48 MP; actual dimensions are recorded with each item. Flash, physical lens selection, tap focus/exposure, exposure bias, zoom, custom exposure and white balance use public capability checks. The UI provides a timer, grid, manual photo ISO/shutter and white-balance channel gains. ISO and shutter slider limits come from the active format; color gain limits come from the device. Automatic reset restores focus, exposure and white balance. Entering video mode restores automatic exposure so a long manual photo shutter does not contradict the requested frame rate.

Video uses `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput` and optional `AVCaptureAudioDataOutput`. The writer is initialized with a content type, **without a file URL**. Apple HLS fragmented MPEG-4 output uses a two-second preferred segment interval and H.264 or HEVC SDR encoding. The first video timestamp starts the writer session and its segment interval. Audio samples before this point are dropped. Real-time input backpressure drops samples instead of accumulating a queue. A writer callback synchronously encrypts each initialization/media segment before its only app-owned durable write. Each segment is capped at 32 MiB. Save errors stop capture and cancel the incomplete asset. Finishing succeeds only after the writer has completed and the vault has committed an authenticated segment manifest. The small video cover is generated in memory and encrypted separately.

Available video choices are the intersection of exact device format dimensions, supported frame-rate ranges and writer output settings. The current selection includes 720p/1080p/4K, 24/25/30/60/100/120 fps, H.264 and HEVC where advertised. Entering video mode configures the actual device format, frame duration and output dimensions before the controller advertises the selection. Preview-sized buffers are disabled for this video preview as well as recording. Each recorded buffer is checked against the selected dimensions before encoding, and in-flight frames captured before the recording boundary are discarded. Stopping recording removes the recording audio configuration without switching the preview back to a photo preset. These checks are compatibility checks, not proof that every combination will run reliably on every device. Microphone encoding is currently mono AAC at 48 kHz / 128 kbps. Standard stabilization is requested only if supported.

## Preview, controls and orientation

The camera uses a portrait interface-orientation mask. Its chrome and preview bounds do not rearrange when the phone is held sideways. The app restores portrait and landscape support for Photos, Search, Settings and media viewing, and reapplies the appropriate mask after camera/library navigation, unlock and scene activation. Locking returns to the portrait lock screen. Interface orientation does not determine the saved media orientation.

The preview layer stays at a fixed 90-degree rotation in portrait UI coordinates. `AVCaptureDevice.RotationCoordinator` supplies a lens-aware physical capture angle; `UIDevice` orientation supplies icon rotation and a deterministic capture fallback. Capture angles are quantized to matching quarter-turns for photo metadata, movie transforms and derived video covers. Unknown, face-up and face-down readings retain the last stable orientation. Orientation observers are removed on stop, and callbacks from an earlier session or lens are rejected. A movie's transform is fixed when recording begins; later rotation changes control icons but does not rewrite an in-progress movie's transform.

Photo mode frames a 3:4 viewfinder and reserves space below it for the mode row and shutter. The zoom strip can overlay the bottom of the image. Video uses a 9:16 viewfinder behind the lower controls. Layout preserves these aspect ratios inside the available viewport, including compact heights, rather than stretching the preview into an arbitrary rectangle. Sensor format, stabilization and front-camera mirroring still require hardware checks; layout tests cannot establish sensor field of view.

The rear zoom strip names real native lens positions, while a separate button switches front/rear cameras. Compatible rear configurations use the system virtual camera and ramp its zoom factor; formats that require a physical camera use that input instead. The system can select a constituent lens based on scene conditions. Selection feedback animates without saving preview snapshots. Telephoto labels derive from available device zoom factors, falling back to field-of-view ratios, rather than assuming that every phone has a 4× lens. Front and rear video preferences are held separately in memory. A supported prior selection is retained; otherwise the rear preference is 4K60 HEVC and the front preference is 1080p30 HEVC, with supported H.264 or capability-list fallbacks. An unavailable combination is never shown as the applied mode. Mode/lens changes disable conflicting controls while the serial capture queue reconfigures the session. Initial startup and changing settings use the same configuration path; failure recovery must be exercised on hardware.

Continuous autofocus and subject-area monitoring are enabled when supported. Tap-to-focus starts a single autofocus adjustment, presents a brief focus reticle and returns to continuous autofocus. Camera/mode changes restore automatic focus; stale observation callbacks are rejected. Focus response and transition quality still require hardware checks. Exposure uses one-third-stop steps in the settings sheet; manual photo exposure/color controls have a separate page.

High-rate video choices are exact-format and encoder gated. 4K100/120 uses HEVC. An explicit supported high-rate choice is retained; default/fallback selection stays at 60 fps or below. This does not certify sustained frame delivery under thermal pressure. RAW photos have a direct control when the current output supports them.

## Playback and lifecycle

`SecureVideoPlayback` retains the player, custom-scheme asset, and resource-loader delegate. The resource loader supplies the MPEG-4 UTI, exact authenticated length and byte-range support. Requests honor `currentOffset`, finite requested ranges, and requests-to-end without overflowing integer arithmetic. It returns at most 256 KiB per queue turn, allowing cancellation between chunks. The vault reader verifies segment hash, AES-GCM context, index and plaintext length before returning a range, caching at most one decrypted segment. It never creates a decrypted movie file. AVPlayer can seek by requesting different byte ranges. AirPlay/external playback is disabled.

On lock, the vault synchronously revokes all readers and writers. The app stops capture, clears sensitive UI, and tears down the player. Interrupted or backgrounded recordings are discarded. Completed encrypted assets remain intact. Camera runtime errors and interruptions abort active capture; normal interruption end can restart the preview if it is still desired. Frameworks may keep private buffers; Swift/AVFoundation memory cannot be guaranteed zeroized. The no-plaintext-disk claim covers app-owned writes, not undocumented OS internals.

## Deliberately unavailable

Dolby Vision/HDR, Log/Log 2, ProRes/ProRes RAW, Live Photos, spatial audio, stereo microphone selection, external-drive recording, depth/portrait auxiliary data, and automatic framing/pose guidance are not advertised as working features. They need format-specific implementation and device validation without violating the encrypted path. No paid Apple Camera-only feature or future SDK capability is assumed.

ProRes RAW is a public iOS API, but it is not implemented by this recorder. It requires RAW source buffers, locked-white-balance metadata and a compatible writer/container. The current `.mpeg4AppleHLS` segment path does not permit ProRes RAW; a file-output workaround would violate the app-owned encrypted-write policy. RAW video needs a separately validated capture, bounded buffering, cover and playback path.

## Verification

`VideoPipelineTests` generates pixel buffers in memory, encodes actual H.264 fragments, authenticates the encrypted manifest, checks that only `.vlt` files exist, loads the encrypted resource through AVFoundation, checks its video track and duration, and verifies reader revocation. A second test rejects empty recordings. Vault tests separately cover altered/swapped segments, range boundaries and session lock.

`CameraConfigurationTests` covers supported mode selection, per-camera preference handling, actual zoom-label values, physical orientation mappings, quarter-turn normalization, locked-session rejection and encrypted movie transform preservation. The format and lens selection tests use synthetic capability lists; they do not assert that a particular iPhone supports a mode. `InterfaceLayoutTests` checks viewfinder aspect ratios, bounds and reserved photo-control space. It also renders real Photos/Search/camera views with an empty locked vault at compact portrait, standard portrait and landscape viewport sizes, including large search text. It checks visible headings, removed slogans and horizontal overflow, and writes synthetic PNGs to `Documents/UITestSnapshots` with retained XCTest attachments. Camera screenshots deliberately do not start a camera session. These are rendered interface checks, not hardware screenshots or full VoiceOver tests. Use the matching CI test summary for actual pass/fail status.

Physical-device release gates remain mandatory:

1. Capture and view HEIF, JPEG and each available RAW type, verify metadata, actual dimensions and unchanged encoded originals.
2. Record at least 30 seconds in every offered lens/resolution/fps/codec combination; play audio, inspect rotation, and seek near the beginning/middle/end. Repeat portrait and both landscape orientations; front-camera output is not promised to match mirrored preview.
3. Inspect app container during capture, playback and seeking for plaintext media; inspect file protection and backup exclusion.
4. Lock, background, interrupt with calls, revoke microphone/camera access, and terminate during capture. Confirm only incomplete ciphertext is removed and no item is committed without a validated manifest.
5. Exercise low storage, encoder failure, segment corruption, long recording memory, thermal pressure and repeated record/stop/lens-switch cycles. Confirm every failure preserves prior assets.
6. Confirm capture/search/playback in airplane mode after initial installation. System photo-picker iCloud behavior is separate from capture.
7. Keep the camera open and rotate through portrait, both landscapes, upside down and flat-on-table positions. Chrome must keep its portrait geometry; icons should turn without restarting the session. Capture in each stable direction and compare full-resolution photo orientation, movie track transform and encrypted cover orientation. Rotate during a recording and confirm that its starting transform remains fixed.
8. While already holding the phone in landscape, open Photos from the camera and return through the floating camera button. Photos and the full-screen viewer should rotate; returning to the camera should restore fixed portrait geometry. Repeat with a presented settings sheet, after background/unlock, and with system rotation lock on and off. Confirm no orientation transition uncovers a locked view.
9. Change photo/video modes and every available rear lens, then front and back, before recording. The preview aspect and applied format should match the selected mode before the shutter is pressed. Verify per-side remembered selections, supported fallback behavior and unchanged preview mode after stopping a movie. Stress rapid taps and interruptions during reconfiguration; a failed configuration must not leave an advertised mode that differs from actual buffers.
